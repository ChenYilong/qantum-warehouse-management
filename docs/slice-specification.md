# First-slice specification

Slice: **go/no-go per site for team leaders, with a recorded response to every shortfall.** Boundary and routes: `slice-decisions.md`. Architecture: `technical-design.md`. Vocabulary: `glossary.md`. On any conflict between this document and the design, correct this one first and then the design.

## 1. Purpose

Before dispatching a crew, a leader must confirm that stock covers every material the planned penetrations consume, and settle each gap. The slice answers "can the crew go?" and, whenever the answer is no, stores what the leader decided.

**Users.** Primary: the team leader. Secondary, as recipients of the output rather than users: purchasing, the warehouse, and whichever manager reviews a substitute.

**Value.** No more checking stock by phone call or spreadsheet. Each shortage is surfaced once, with a stored decision that crew planning can depend on.

## 2. Scope

**Included:** site list; readiness per site; shortfalls plus blockers caused by absent data; pooled stock per material against every site's demand; storing wait, escalate and substitute proposals; listing stored actions; substitutes suggested by catalogue similarity. Later rounds added:

- Field-use round (18 Oct 2026): connectivity shown on all screens; offline operation; text or phone a clear site's operator; blocked sites open on their pending decisions.
- Signal round (18 Oct): one large green or red toggle per site that dials the operator, and messaging the operator about missing parts so no trip is wasted.
- On-site review round (19 Oct): a second, on-site check of every picked material, plan match or not, with findings (unsuitable, wrong item, damaged) sent to the warehouse for re-picking or delivery that day.
- Project filter (19 Oct): narrowing projects by readiness and by free text, borrowed from how qantum-stock lists work packages.
- Project maps (19 Oct): a map per project, directions from the device, and drive distance and time.
- Header round (19 Oct): Online switch relocated to the pinned header, theme picker relocated to the Guide, and the phone tab bar used on wide screens.
- Map position (19 Oct): each project map sits under the project name and above the signal, at the signal's width.
- Fallback origin (19 Oct): until the device shares its position, distances and drive times are measured from a fixed origin, Auckland city warehouse.
- Daily panel (20 Oct): a verdict for today heads the list (crews that cannot leave; one bar segment apiece), then tabs All / Blocked / "Crew can go" / Other and a map for whichever tab is active, under which a project reappears as its card from the list; derived from the author's design "I · Verdict + tabs + map (B+D+E)" (exported from a design tool; not in the repo).
- Deadline (20 Oct): every project has a due date, printed in English at the bottom of its card.
- Deadline calendar (20 Oct): a second panel cloned from the daily one, swapping the map for a month view: projects drawn on their due dates as small signals, and a date range limiting which ones show.
- Tabs (20 Oct): the tab bar becomes Map, Calendar, Projects (later with Guide); Map receives the daily panel, Calendar the month view, and Projects retains its filter and cards.

**Excluded (section 8):** production authentication; production inventory or nomination feeds; substitute approval; cross-site stock reservation; scheduling of crews; offline data and decision queues; installation, pins, photos.

| FR3 | Demand is compared with on-hand stock: the total over all of the leader's locations, pooled between sites, and labelled unreserved. |

## 4. Acceptance criteria

Numbered so tests can carry the number in their names. Section 7 defines the sample data.

**"Readiness"**
1. Site A: two penetrations need 12 and 8 of material M, and stock is 15. Result: 20 required, 15 available, 5 missing; crew blocked.
2. With every material covered, the crew is clear.
3. M stocked at two locations holding 6 and 9 reads on hand 15.
4. A demand of 2.2 units is shown as 3. Stock figures get the same snapping: ten rows of 0.1 satisfy a demand of 1; 4.2 available for a demand of 5 leaves 0.8 missing.
5. No stock row: the material becomes an unknown shortage, figures null; wait and escalate both succeed. Negative or non-finite stock rows, or a non-finite total, likewise turn the material unknown.
6. A nominated code that the catalogue lacks produces blocker `unknown_solution_code` and a blocked crew; wait gets 422, escalate succeeds.
7. A nominated code without material mapping produces blocker `no_material_mapping` and a blocked crew. Mapped quantities below zero or non-finite produce blocker `invalid_quantity`; that penetration contributes no demand, the crew is blocked, and the value can never offset another penetration's need.
8. A site with zero penetrations reads "nothing planned", never clear.
9. On a stock-service failure the API returns 502, and the UI displays the agreed can't-check banner ("Can't check this site right now. Don't assume it's clear. Try again."), and no clear status appears anywhere; the site list then shows that site with the amber "Can't check" signal (AC 52).
10. Two sites each requiring 10 of M, with 15 available, both read clear, and the figure is labelled "on hand, shared, not reserved".

**Decisions**
11. A wait on a live shortage returns 201; the shortage becomes waiting and the crew stays blocked.
12. Escalation needs `escalateTo` set to purchasing or warehouse; absent or invalid → 422.
13. Acting on a shortage absent at request time → 404.
14. Escalate followed by wait: state = escalated, both entries listed.
15. An action stored at shortfall 5, with the shortfall now 8, reads "earlier decision, shortfall has grown", and the shortage is open.
16. A replayed POST (same key, same target) returns the stored record with 200 and writes nothing, also after the shortage resolves or the nomination changes. That key aimed at another target → 409 `idempotency_key_reused`. A POST lacking a key → 400. Uniqueness of keys is per user.
17. Notes or reasons over 500 characters once trimmed → 422; a reason made only of whitespace is refused. Bodies above 10,000 bytes → 413, enforced during streaming with or without `Content-Length`.

**Substitution**
18. `0438` nominated (wall, PEX Ø25mm, rating 60/30): candidates `0451` and `0464`.
19. `0789` → `0790`, `0791`. `0434` → `0435`.
20. `0344` → no candidates; an empty state explains this. Escalate appears only for a penetration with a data problem; its sealant shortage is handled where sealant is, on that material's page.
21. `0943` (truncated substrate) → none, with a message blaming the incomplete catalogue entry.
22. A candidate without an insulation claim is never offered against a stated insulation requirement.
23. A `to` code outside the live candidates → 422. A `from` code other than the live nomination → 409.
24. A stored proposal has status proposed and keeps its reason; nomination and shortage are unchanged.
25. Applying the matching rules (rating of each solution as its requirement, truncated substrates left out), 20 of 148 solutions have candidates, no more, no fewer; the test breaks if that number moves.

**Data and safety**
26. The catalogue loader reads all 148 rows, keeps raw strings, and flags 6 whose substrate is truncated; the check breaks if either count moves.
27. Each sample penetration points at an `internal_code` that the catalogue contains, apart from the single planted missing code.
28. Database credentials never reach the client.
29. "Readiness" and actions responses send `Cache-Control: no-store`.

**Experience**
30. Each shortage names its material, quantities needed, held and missing, the penetrations that plan it, and its state; no item relies on colour.
31. Every control works from the keyboard; interactive targets measure ≥ 48px (raised from 44px during the field-use round).
32. At 375px width no content requires sideways scrolling.
33. A candidate needing a material that this site already lacks gets availability `short`, even when one install alone would fit in present stock. Unknown site stock for one of its materials makes it `unknown`. Shortages in unrelated materials leave a candidate whose own needs fit on hand at `in_stock`.
34. A blocked site screen carries no summary banner; it starts with the signal plus operator row (AC 57), with the "Needs your decision" panel (AC 50) next. Three chips (shortages, data problems, acted) give penetration counts and narrow the rows. "Acted" includes a penetration if it has a substitute proposal, or a wait/escalation still in force against a shortage it belongs to or against its own data problem; earlier or resolved decisions still appear as row marks (AC 44) but are not counted. Each row keeps its own line. On a clear site the signal still says "Crew can go." (AC 52), and the signal on the sites list still states the counts of shortages and data problems.
35. A `solution_mismatch` blocker, naming failing fields, arises if the two disagree on orientation; if, once normalised, substrate, service type or size disagree; or if the solution's integrity or insulation is below a stated requirement. Consequences: crew blocked; wait → 422; escalate accepted; no material demand from that penetration. Differences of spacing or letter case alone are no mismatch. A substrate truncated after its family name never fits, whichever side has it, even against an identically truncated value. Blank values, and requirements or ratings that are not usable numbers, can never fit.
36. The penetration page pairs every field of the penetration with the same field of its nominated solution and flags each misfit with an icon plus words, not colour only.
37. These pages show a bottom-anchored tab bar, a single navigation landmark called Main, at any width: sites list, Map, Calendar, materials list, actions log, plus the landing page (the Guide, `/about-this-demo`; `/` redirects there). Entries: Map, Calendar, Projects, Guide (AC 83); the active entry is highlighted; it does not move on scroll, and the page reserves space for it so no content sits underneath. Theme selection (System | Light | Dark, where System mirrors the OS; the choice persists on this device and is applied before first paint) is on the Guide, not in the header. Deeper screens show no tab bar and put a back control first; a screen never has both.
38. Materials list: each material planned anywhere, alphabetical, showing stock held (or unknown), total demand over all sites, and the count of short sites. Unchecked sites never count as not short and cause the combined figure to be hidden. If every site alone is fine but together they exceed stock, the row states that rather than "not short" (pooled, unreserved stock).
39. Material page: pooled stock with its age, stated once; then a section for each planning site, showing demand, gap (or "not short for this site alone"), places (duplicates merged), and for short sites only the scope of a decision, Wait, Escalate, and a warning if a previous decision predates a larger gap. Decisions themselves appear under the page's Decisions tab rather than as a state label. Unchecked sites state that they are unchecked; they never appear sufficient.
40. Penetration page: every shortage touching the nominated solution (or a substitute whose material this site already lacks) gets one line of local figures ("this site short 2 of 4", or "this site needs 1" if stock is unknown), linking into that site's part of the material page.
41. Old per-site material URLs redirect to the matching section of the material page.
42. When opened via a penetration's shortage line, the material page's back control leads to that penetration. Any other entry, or a `from` naming no penetration that uses the material, sends back to Materials.
43. The Decisions page (`/actions`) is split into per-site sections, ordered like the sites. A section starts with a site link, then decisions followed by proposals, newest on top, each dated, attributed and marked as applying or not. A material decision links to that site's part of the material page; data-problem decisions and proposals link to their penetration; resolved decisions have no link. If a site's log fails to load the section says so instead of appearing empty; if no site can be read the page is unavailable. Site headings show the site icon and stay pinned while their entries scroll beneath. Pages entered from the log (site, penetration, material) offer back to the log; entered otherwise, or with an unknown origin, back leads to the usual parent. No actions-log button exists on site or penetration pages; legacy `/sites/<id>/actions` opens the log scrolled to that site.
44. Each penetration row on the site screen carries icon marks within its link: problem kinds first (numbered when repeated), then escalations, waits and substitute proposals, always numbered, using the same counting as the penetration's Decisions tab so totals agree. If decisions fail to load, rows carry problem marks only, and the list explains why rather than showing 0. Each mark has full wording (e.g. "Short material × 2") exposed to assistive tech and as a tooltip; shapes as well as colours tell marks apart. The row remains a single link; marks are not controls.
45. Penetration page tabs, implemented as links: Solution (default) and Decisions (`?tab=log`); the active one is marked. Solution contains the nominated solution and the substitutes. The Decisions tab lists, most recent at the top: waits and escalations on this site's shortages of any material the nominated solution consumes (resolved included); those on the penetration's own data problem; and its substitute proposals. Every entry carries a date, an author and its current validity. Whilst the shortage lasts, a material decision says it spans all N penetrations here, with a link to the material page; back from there lands on this tab. A count appears on the tab; with an unreadable log the number is omitted and "nothing recorded" never appears. Tab links preserve the log origin (`fromLog`).
46. The material page offers two tabs as links, Stock (default) and Decisions (`?tab=log`), both keeping `from` and `fromLog`. Decisions here: every wait and escalation for this material's shortage across sites, newest first, labelled by site, not linked; proposals are excluded. The tab count is shown only if all site logs loaded. Sites whose log failed are listed in a warning, their decisions omitted; with every log failing, the tab reports it cannot load. Neither case ever shows 0 or "nothing recorded".
47. At 1024px and wider, pages match the phone layout: identical top header, identical bottom tab bar (entries kept within the 45rem column), and neither side menu nor collapse toggle. Inner pages retain back navigation; sticky headings pin directly beneath the header. `/` redirects (temporarily) to `/about-this-demo`.

**Field use (2026-10-18)**
48. Each screen's header, pinned to the top while scrolling, carries the Online switch at its right end (the former place of the theme control); the first line below the header is the picker's latest action (the stock feed time, or "unknown"), and error screens get a header too, for the switch. Being Online requires three things: `navigator.onLine`, a successful `GET /api/health` (every 30 s, again on reconnect and on tab visibility), and the toggle in its on position. Before the first reply it reads Checking, not Online; after re-enabling, Online returns only with a server reply. When off or unreachable it shows Offline with the time the device last reached the server; a bottom banner gives the data's age and opens "Offline details" (last online; the age of this screen and of the stock figures; anything queued; "Sync now" with the time of the previous sync); decision dialogs refuse to send and say why. "Sync now" calls the server, reloads the view and stamps the time; if it fails it states that nothing was sent. Announcements happen only for real changes. During a stock feed outage the health check still passes, just without a timestamp.
49. On the sites list every site is a card: site name linking to the site; signal (AC 52); actions. Layout is a masonry flow: two columns filled top-down from 672px (cards keep natural height; each column still gives the signal 280px), one column below that. Wherever an operator is assigned, "Text operator" (message prepared per AC 55) and "Call operator" appear in any status other than nothing planned. Blocked sites additionally offers "Sort out shortages", leading to its "Needs your decision" panel, or "Check data problems" if it has no shortage. On an uncheckable site, tapping the signal re-runs the check. Nothing-planned sites offer no action. Action labels never include the site name. Optionally, a site names an operator and a picker: name and international phone number; sample numbers come from Ofcom's drama range and reach nobody.
50. Blocked site screens begin with the "Needs your decision" panel: "Can't go yet" with the counts of shortages and data problems; per shortage, the figure with its unit ("Short 2 of 10 cartridges", or "Stock unknown · needs 1 tube"), a link to affected penetrations ("4 penetrations affected"), Wait, Escalate; the data problems with a link filtering to their penetrations; the effect of each option; materials this site alone has enough of, with the pooled-stock caveat; and contacts for picker and operator, merged into one entry when it is the same person. The panel contains nothing that unblocks the crew. Clear and nothing-planned sites have no panel.
51. Body copy 18px, headings scaled to match; the 48px target rule (AC 31) holds on every 375px-wide screen, which never scrolls sideways.

**Signal (2026-10-18)**
52. The signal has four states, separated by colour, knob shape and mark, and knob side, so colour is never the only cue. Go: green, a ticked circle, knob right, an "I" at the left end; clear sites only. Blocked: red, stop-sign icon, knob on the left, an "O" on the right. Can't check: amber, warning-triangle icon, knob on the left; used too for any status outside the known set. Nothing planned: grey, dashed-ring icon, knob on the left. The label is the status text: "Crew can go", "Blocked · 2 shortages", "Can't check", "Nothing planned". Go is reserved for clear sites.
53. Both themes: label and knob contrast ≥ 7:1 against the fill; edge contrast ≥ 3:1 against both page and card. Hue alone already separates go (green) from blocked (red).
54. Where the site is clear or blocked and has an operator, tapping the signal dials the operator (`tel:`, the same link as "Call operator"). The signal never opens a text message. On an uncheckable site a tap re-checks and never calls. Nothing planned, or no operator: the signal ignores taps; without an operator it reads "No operator assigned".
55. The prepared operator message names the site and job ref and varies by state. Clear: retains "shared, not reserved" and confirms the crew may go. Blocked: parts NOT complete; hold off; at most three short materials, then how many others; how many data problems; a promise to report back once sorted; it never tells the crew to go. Uncheckable: materials cannot be checked, do not go until confirmed. Clear, blocked and uncheckable sites with an operator show "Text operator" and "Call operator" next to the signal, so the operator hears before any wasted trip.
56. For blocked sites `GET /api/sites` includes `shortages`, each entry giving the material's id, its name and unit, a kind (short|unknown), the required amount and the shortfall (or null), sorted as readiness sorts them. Non-blocked sites get an empty array; its length always equals `shortageCount`.
57. Signal height ≥ 88px, fitting a 375px viewport without horizontal scroll. A blocked site's card on the list names at most three short materials and counts the others. Heading the site screen, signal and operator row are placed ahead of "Needs your decision"; on clear or nothing-planned sites the signal replaces the banner. The signal's accessible name avoids the site name and the labels of the two contact buttons.

**Online colours; demo sign-in (19 Oct 2026)**
58. When online the header control is green, its text dark; offline, red with white text (each ≥ 4.5:1); while checking it uses the header's black. Its wording (Online / Offline) persists, so the state never depends on colour alone.
59. A first visit, or any signed-out session, shows a sign-in covering the whole viewport; the page beneath is inert. Fields: username, password and a role choice (Team leader / Operator, defaulting to Team leader), plus "Forgot password" and "Create account". Submitting without a username or password names the missing field and keeps the overlay; when both are filled it closes, accepting any password. The header then shows "Hi, <username>" in place of "Team leader", and reloading keeps the session without the overlay flashing up first.
60. "Sign out" sits at the top right of the Guide; it reopens the sign-in with the username prefilled. "Create account" asks for username, email (optional), role and a password entered twice that must agree, then signs in. "Forgot password" accepts username or email, and explains no message goes out in the demo. Username, role, accounts (minus passwords) and signed-in state persist in this browser only.

**Picking: check and error reports (19 Oct 2026)**
61. Per line, the pick check sets planned quantity, spec and parts against the actual pick; the warehouse's "Picked" status plays no role. Issue types: picked spec ≠ planned spec (wrong spec); main items complete but a required part lacking (marked picked, parts missing); fewer items than planned (partly picked). One issue suffices to make the site's picking not ready. Quantities below zero, non-finite or missing, and a missing spec where the plan names one, block as well; a site without a pick list cannot be checked. Neither case is ever ready.
62. The site screen's Picks section shows the outcome, "Not ready · N pick issues" with "Review pick issues", "Picks match the plan", or "Can't check picks"; the picker (name marked "(sample)", role, latest action, phone, email) with Call (`tel:`) and Email (`mailto:`); and for each material the needed and picked amounts plus a label ("Matches plan", "Part-picked", "Missing parts", "Wrong spec"). "Crew can go" unlocks only with picking matched, the site clear and the device online; then it sends the operator a message.
63. Without a connection the Picks section reads "Can't confirm · offline" with the last known issue count; each label becomes "Was …"; "Crew can go" stays locked with "Needs a live check. Never decided from saved data."; Call keeps working; queued reports are counted.
64. The "Pick check" page (`/sites/<id>/picks`) has one card per problem line: wrong spec: planned and actual specs in two columns; missing parts, with picked-of-needed per part; partly picked: progress bar of picked versus needed, and any accessory not picked. It notes that the warehouse status cannot override the check, gives the picker with a Call action, and offers "Report to warehouse" plus "Wait for re-pick" (which saves nothing). When offline it labels the issues as the last known.
65. The report page (`/sites/<id>/picks/report`) collects: kind (wrong spec, missing parts, partial pick, other); material, chosen from this site's pick lines; both amounts (planned, picked); which part is absent; up to 500 characters of note; an optional photo (left on the device); recipients, being picker, purchasing or both (one at minimum). `POST /api/sites/<id>/pick-reports` with an `Idempotency-Key` stores it (201); repeating the key returns the stored one (200); an unknown line → 404 `pick_line_not_found`; any invalid input → 422.
66. With no connection, "Send report" parks the report on the device, key included, and tells the user; after reconnecting it is sent automatically, and resending can never store it twice. Queued reports are counted on the site screen.

**Site review (2026-10-19)**
67. On the site screen, Materials · planned vs picked gives every row a dedicated Review button, independent of the label ("Matches plan", "Part-picked", "Missing parts", "Wrong spec"): named Review, described by its material, ≥ 48px, available online and offline. The material name remains a link to its "Pick check" card. Review navigates to `/sites/<id>/picks/review?line=<line>`, displaying needed and picked quantity plus label, then asking for the site's verdict: "Yes, it's right" or "No, something's wrong".
68. "Yes, it's right" stores a review of the line via `POST /api/sites/<id>/pick-reviews` with an `Idempotency-Key`: 201; repeating the key → original (200); unknown line → 404 `pick_line_not_found`; key reused on a different line → 409; any other body content → 422. The line then shows "Checked on site" plus the time beneath its label. Reviews never converts a mismatched line into a match.
69. "No, something's wrong" loads the report form for that line with kind left empty, forcing the leader to name the finding; "Doesn't fit", "Wrong item" and "Damaged" join the four kinds from AC 65. Further fields: number affected (optional whole number, 0–100000) and the need, either picking again for the following delivery (default choice) or a drop that same day. `POST /api/sites/<id>/pick-reports` accepts the new kinds, `affectedQty` and `request` (`repick` or `same_day`; omitted means re-pick); anything else → 422.
70. If a line's newest site report is more recent than its newest review, it is a pick issue ("Reported on site"): picking for the site is not ready, and "Send the crew" remains locked. The row states the finding, the number and the request; a line that already has an issue keeps its label and gets this line added. On the "Pick check" page it gets a card showing the report and its timestamp; all cards carry Review. A more recent review closes the report, and the line is then judged by the pick list alone. Offline the label reads "Was reported on site".
71. Offline, "Yes, it's right" holds the review on the device with its idempotency key, as for reports, and sends it automatically once, and only once, after reconnecting. The site screen counts queued reviews and reports separately ("1 report and 1 review waiting to send").

**Projects filter (2026-10-19)**

72. The projects list (`/sites`) begins with a filter form: "Readiness" with "All statuses", Ready, Shortage, Unknown. Ready: only projects with a crew free to go. Shortage: only blocked ones. Unknown: nothing planned plus uncheckable. "All statuses": everything. Projects that cannot be checked are excluded from Ready and Shortage; unrecognised statuses count as Unknown. The selection lives in the URL as `status` (`/sites?status=shortage`); absent, unknown or repeated values mean "All statuses".
73. Also in the form: "Search projects" (over name, job ref, address; case-insensitive; trimmed; max 100 characters; URL parameter `q`), an Apply button, and Reset linking to `/sites`; it is a plain GET form that works without JavaScript. With a filter active the list shows "N of M projects", or "No projects match these filters." when empty, and Reset restores everything. All controls have labels and ≥ 48px targets; at 375px the page is axe-clean and does not scroll sideways.

**Project maps (2026-10-19)**

74. `GET /api/sites` includes each site's `location` (`{ lat, lng }`; invented Auckland coordinates in the sample). Valid location means: finite values; latitude in −90…90; longitude in −180…180; not 0,0. Such projects get a 48px "Directions" link (accessible name "Directions"; its description is the project name) to Google Maps routing from device to point and, given a public Mapbox token, a pinned map of the point placed under the project name and over the signal, at the signal's exact width. The map uses Mapbox GL JS, loads only as its card nears the viewport, and cannot pan or zoom; a tap opens those directions as well. Invalid or absent location: map, "Directions" and distance are all omitted. Without a token: no map, while "Directions" and distance keep working.
75. Each project displays distance and drive time, computed in one request to the Mapbox driving matrix that covers all visible projects. Before the device reports a position, routes begin at the fallback origin, Auckland city warehouse (coordinates −36.8643, 174.7661 taken from OpenStreetMap), which the line names ("2.3 km · 7 min drive from Auckland city warehouse"); this applies before any tap, while locating, and after a refusal or failure. At the top of each tab page a line states the origin of distances, with the reason when the device position is missing ("Location is off, so distances are from Auckland city warehouse."; "Can't find where you are right now, so distances are from Auckland city warehouse."). "Use my location" requests the device position only on a tap, or automatically if permission already exists; once known it replaces the fallback, the line drops the place name ("12.3 km · 18 min drive"), and the button becomes "Update my location". The shown result is only replaced once a fresh one arrives; until the first arrives the line reads "Working out the drive…". When no route exists (no token, offline, Mapbox error, no road found), the line falls back to crow-flies distance from that origin and states that drive time is unavailable ("9.8 km in a straight line · no drive time"; from the fallback, "9.8 km in a straight line from Auckland city warehouse · no drive time"). Durations are never made up. The device position is shared with Mapbox alone, only following that tap or a prior permission, and is not persisted.
76. Mapbox access uses a public token only. `MAPBOX_PUBLIC_TOKEN` must begin with `pk.`: an `sk.` token aborts the build, deployment refuses an `sk.` value stored as the GitHub secret, and other values simply switch maps off. The token is not exposed as a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variable and is absent from the browser bundle; the projects page passes it to the map at render time.
77. On the Map tab (`/map`, AC 83) a verdict panel follows the page h1 (Map; visually hidden, because the panel comes first; the page is marked in the tab bar). A crew = a project with planned work; it cannot go if blocked or if its stock cannot be checked (fail closed). First line of the panel: the active tab's name, then "today is" and the browser's current date in English ("Map · today is Tuesday 20 October 2026"); then the totals; then, as a large h2, the verdict, always white on the app's black: "2 of 3 crews can't go yet" when any crew cannot go, "3 of 3 crews can go" when all can, "No crews have work planned" with no crew (singular: "1 of 1 crew …"). Totals add up shortages and data problems across projects ("3 shortages · 4 data problems", "1 shortage · no data problems", "No shortages · no data problems"), replaced by "Stock can't be checked" as soon as any crew's stock is uncheckable. Beneath the verdict a bar has a segment per crew, can-go crews first: green (can go), red with a bright edge (blocked), yellow (cannot be checked), each clearly visible on the black panel, and described in words ("1 can go, 2 blocked"). With zero crews no bar is drawn. Panel text contrast ≥ 4.5:1. The verdict counts every project; the list filter has no effect on it.
78. Directly below the bar sit four tabs: "All 4", "Blocked 2", "Crew can go 1", "Other 1". All = every project, unfiltered; Blocked = every crew unable to go yet (blocked plus uncheckable); "Crew can go" = crews free to leave; Other = projects without planned work. Tablist semantics: a single tab stop; arrow keys plus Home/End move and select; each tab ≥ 48 × 48. Initial tab: "Crew can go", or Blocked if that is empty, then Other. The selected tab's bar segments are fully opaque and the rest dimmed (All lights everything; Other has no segments, so everything dims).
79. Below the panel comes the tab content: a map holding just the active tab's projects (everything under All), each drawn as a pin button (≥ 48 × 48, named by project and status, coloured like its signal, captioned with the name up to its first comma); the selected pin is larger and pressed. Given a public token, pins appear on a fixed Mapbox map fitted to their extent; without one, or after a load failure, they are positioned relative to each other on a plain backdrop. Immediately below the map is the selected project's card, the same component the list renders for that project (same look and content: name, map, signal, shortages, operator, actions, trip, "Directions"), constrained to the map's width; its ids are distinct from the list's, avoiding duplicates on the page. Switching tab chooses the earliest project of that tab in list order; a pin tap chooses its project. An empty tab shows "No projects in this group." without pins.
80. `GET /api/sites` includes an optional `date` per site, the deadline, as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date (invented in the sample). The usual upstream policy applies (strict type, so a non-string fails the response; lenient value): every list card with a genuine calendar date closes with "Deadline · Fri 23 Oct 2026" (English weekday, day, abbreviated month and year of the stored date exactly, never shifted by time zone, wrapped in a `time` element); the card under the daily map is that component too and shows it as well. ## 5. Non-functional requirements

| Area | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Performance | Production readiness response < 1 s for a site with 200 penetrations. |
| Safety | Fail closed: absent or unreachable data can never yield clear. |
| Security | The DB role may only read and insert on the two action tables, so the database enforces append-only. Server-held credentials; TLS for production traffic; author assigned by the server; schema validation at the boundary; errors limited to `{code, message}`. |
| Privacy | Logs hold ids and codes, never notes or reasons. |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 AA on all screens: contrast, focus order, labelling, no state by colour alone. |
| Maintainability | Domain core free of framework and I/O imports; one port per upstream. |
| Auditability | Append-only actions recording actor and timestamp. |
| Delivery | CI covers typecheck, lint, unit, API, DB and E2E tests; deployment only after a green run. |

## 6. Assumptions

The README repeats each as a known assumption.

1. Each nomination provides orientation, substrate, service type and size of its penetration, plus required integrity and insulation.
2. Per-solution material quantities exist somewhere upstream; here they are fabricated.
3. Per material, stock is one pooled number added up across locations.
4. Escalation recipients are purchasing and the warehouse.
5. Any leader may open any sample site; no notion of site membership exists.
6. One shortage blocks the entire site; partial dispatch comes later.
7. Substitute approval happens in a separate process that this slice does not rely on.
8. The character `:` never appears in site or material ids (shortage id = `siteId:materialId`). Validation happens at the boundary for upstream data, and bad numbers additionally make the domain fail closed.
9. The path to the catalogue is server-side configuration; users never provide it.
10. The stock feed timestamp stands for the picker's latest action, as the warehouse updates stock when it picks; production would also report who picked and what.
11. The planning system supplies operator and picker with each site; here they are fabricated.

## 7. Sample data (fabricated and labelled)

Stored in `data/sample/`, whose README says so. Nothing but the CSV is real.

- **Sites:** at least three, covering clear, shortage-blocked, and missing data.
- **Materials and mappings:** a few (sealant, collar, wrap, …), mapped onto demo codes `0438`, `0789`, `0434`, `0344`, `0334` and their candidates `0451`, `0464`, `0790`, `0791`, `0435`, `0347`.
- **Stock:** one material with plenty, one short, one split over two locations, one with no row at all.
- **Penetrations:** one nominating a code the catalogue lacks; one nominating `0943` (incomplete substrate).
- **Requirements:** each penetration requires exactly the integrity and insulation its nominated solution offers; the exception, `pen-c-02`, demands 90 min insulation versus the 60 of `0435`, so that AC 35 is exercised.

## 8. Exclusions

Production authentication; a live inventory or nomination service; substitute approval; reserving or allocating stock; planning crews; offline data and decision queueing (connectivity is shown and the app's own sends are held, but screens are not cached and decisions not queued); rate limiting; push notifications ("Text operator" opens the leader's own messaging app); installation, pins and photos; suppliers other than Ryanfire; catalogue editing.

## 9. Demo script

1. Start at the landing page, which declares a demo running on invented data. Open Projects from the tab bar: site B is blocked.
2. Site B lists its penetrations without any blocked banner; chips give penetration counts, and stock figures carry the pooled, unreserved label.
3. Go to penetration L3, Riser 2 (nominated `0438`). The sealant line there, "this site short 2 of 10", links into site B's part of the sealant page. Raise it with purchasing and add a note. Under the sealant's Decisions tab the escalation appears for site B; going back reaches the penetration again. Site B remains blocked on the list, because a decision does not add stock.
4. On the same penetration, candidates `0451` and `0464` are labelled not verified; `0451` needs sealant, which the site lacks, and says so. Propose `0451`, giving a reason; the record shows proposed, and everything else stays put.
5. Open the penetration nominated `0344` (L5, Plant room): "no catalogue match"; its sealant shortage is handled on the sealant page. Open `/materials`: pipe collar with 2 in stock against 6 planned in total, short for site B, though site A considered alone is covered.
6. Site C: its Data problems chip reads four; each row names its kind and every penetration page explains, among them two misfitting nominations (L1, Stair core: catalogue entry `0943` with a truncated substrate; L1, Riser 1: insulation of 90 min required against 60 from `0435`). The Shortages chip holds the one penetration with unknown stock; nothing is presented as clear.
7. Back on site B, escalated penetrations carry the escalation mark. On L3, Riser 2, the Decisions tab shows both entries, escalation and proposal, dated and attributed. Open `/actions`: one section per site, holding its decisions.

## 10. Production gaps (also in the README)

Sign-in is a browser-side demo; the server checks no identity or role. No rate limiting. Database TLS encrypts without authenticating the server. Offline support stops at connectivity state plus queued pick reports; screens are not cached and decisions not queued. Upstream systems are stubs; stock is pooled and unreserved; there is no approval workflow.
